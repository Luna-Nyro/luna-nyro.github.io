Glaze

Desktop theme for Cinnamon 6.4 / 6.6

A Material 3 “frosted glass” theme for Cinnamon: a floating, rounded panel split into glass islands, rounded windows, M3 colour roles generated from a single seed colour, and a Material Symbols icon theme. Everything is built from one set of design tokens, so changing the seed re-tints the shell, GTK3, GTK4/libadwaita and the icons together. A companion extension adds real backdrop blur; without it the theme still works with near-opaque surfaces.

  • Shell theme with pill menus, tonal window list and M3 calendar, notifications, OSD, switches and sliders
  • GTK3 theme with 20 px rounded windows (CSD and Muffin frames) plus GTK4/libadwaita palette overrides
  • Material Symbols Rounded icons for ~600 symbolic names, tonal squircle app icons and folders
  • Companion extension: two-pass gaussian blur behind panel islands and popup menus
  • Dark and Light variants; glaze seed rebuilds from any accent, glaze rollback undoes apply

Welcome

Sign-in transition extension for Cinnamon

A Cinnamon extension that plays an animation when you log in and when you unlock the screen. Your wallpaper comes alive: its colours swirl and drift through a GLSL flow field with hue drift and a light sweep while a greeting fades in. Then the desktop opens from the centre through a glowing, organic edge as the colours settle back onto the still wallpaper. It replaces Cinnamon’s black startup fade, and on unlock it picks up from the dimmed lock-screen wallpaper so there is no flash of bare desktop.

  • Login and unlock transitions, each toggleable
  • Time-of-day greeting with date and time
  • Different every run: flow, swirl, hue drift, reveal origin, edge shape and tempo are randomised
  • Multi-monitor aware, with the wallpaper at the correct aspect ratio on each screen
  • Speed and colour-motion settings, plus preview buttons

Tandem

Your Android phone on your Linux desktop

A lightweight alternative to Windows Phone Link, written from scratch. A GTK 4 desktop app shows your phone’s status and notifications next to Messages, Calls and Photos, and a separate window lets you see and control the phone screen. A small background service (tandemd, about 6 MB idle) keeps the link alive, and the Android app explains every permission before asking. Phone and computer talk directly over the local network with TLS 1.3 and certificate pinning, or through an optional self-hosted relay that only forwards end-to-end encrypted data.

  • Read and send SMS, see MMS photos; answer, decline and place calls, talking through the PC over Bluetooth
  • Notifications with reply and action buttons; two-way clipboard sync; recent photos with drag-out
  • Phone screen window to view and control the phone
  • Phone as touchpad and keyboard remote; lock or shut down the PC; share files both ways
  • Experimental: fingerprint unlock of the PC and using the phone as the PC’s speaker

Gaffer

A simple DMX lightboard for Linux

Gaffer controls the brightness and colour of DMX lights from a GTK4/libadwaita window. Save scenes and fade between them, or run automations, either effects like Breathe, Rainbow, Chase, Candle, Colour cycle and Strobe or sequences of scenes, by hand, at a time of day on chosen weekdays, or when Gaffer opens. A built-in soundboard plays sound files and can drive the lights from them. It is aimed at small rigs on one DMX universe through a cheap USB-DMX interface, not at replacing a full lighting console.

  • Per-light brightness, colour, on/off and Identify; master fader and blackout
  • Built-in RGB / RGBW / RGBA / dimmer / LED PAR fixture types, or a custom channel order
  • Enttec DMX USB Pro–style, Open DMX and uDMX interfaces, detected automatically where possible
  • Simulator stage when no interface is connected, with sample lights to try
  • Soundboard pads with light cues (pulse with the sound, flash, scene, blackout); gaffer ctl remote control

autolight

Ambient-light brightness and keyboard backlight daemon

The T1 iBridge exposes an ambient light sensor that nothing on Linux used. autolight is a small user daemon that reads it and drives the panel brightness through a smoothed lux curve with hysteresis, and turns the keyboard backlight on in the dark and off in the light. It pauses when you change brightness yourself, dims on idle, and writes through logind, so it needs no root, no kernel changes and no system service.

  • Flicker-free: spike filter, log-domain smoothing, hysteresis band and stepped ramps
  • Manual override per output, resuming after a timeout or on request
  • Idle dimming that coexists with Cinnamon’s own idle dim and respects idle inhibitors
  • Handles lid close, resume from sleep and a re-enumerated sensor
  • autolightctl for status, toggle, brighter/dimmer bias and a sensor probe; 66 offline tests

brcm43602-fix

Investigating BCM43602 Wi-Fi under Linux

Research project · not fixed

Tools and research notes for making the Broadcom BCM43602 (brcmfmac) in this MacBook Pro join 2.4 GHz networks and transmit at a normal rate. The tools recover the card’s own calibration from its SROM, decode it into NVRAM and A/B-test candidates on the real card with automatic rollback. After about 45 candidate NVRAMs, the findings point at the firmware rather than a missing value. Everything is published so others don’t have to repeat the work.

  • Ranged, resumable fetch of only the Broadcom kexts (~30 MB) from a 1.69 GB macOS update
  • Read-only SROM dump through PCI BAR0 and a decoder to name=value NVRAM text
  • Candidate-NVRAM lab that scans, optionally joins, records results and always restores
  • Installer with backups, a self-reverting timer and a boot failsafe; matching rollback script
  • Offline test suite: no root, no hardware, no network beyond localhost
